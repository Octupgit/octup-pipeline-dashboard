Venu X1 Pixel Faces

Venu X1 Pixel Faces

Eight watch faces for the Garmin Venu X1, drawn entirely in a 5×7 pixel font with segmented bars and hard edges. Rendered here at the real 448 × 486 panel size from the same coordinates and the same glyph table as the Monkey C source — this is not an impression of the design, it is the design.

448 × 486 px, rectangular 2.0 in AMOLED venux1 build target type: 5×7 blocks, no font resource 8.8 rects per glyph always-on: off

01  Vanguard

Minimal

Vanguard

Hours over minutes at twenty-pixel blocks — far larger than any built-in font reaches, because every glyph is drawn as rectangles rather than typeset. Everything else is one row and one segmented bar.

Pick this one if you mostly want to know the time. The step goal is the twelve-cell bar, which you read as a count rather than a length.

  • Steps + 12-cell goal bar
  • Temperature
  • Watch battery
  • Minutes accent

vanguard/source/VanguardView.mc

02  Flux

Dashboard

Flux

Six metrics on a rectangular grid, which is what the panel actually is, plus weather, a twenty-cell step bar and today's daylight window.

Pick this one if you open glance widgets several times a day. It is the only face carrying all six activity metrics at once, and the only one with sunrise and sunset times.

  • Steps
  • Heart rate
  • Body Battery
  • Calories
  • Floors
  • Intensity minutes
  • Weather
  • Sun times / move bar

flux/source/FluxView.mc

03  Orbit

Segmented rings

Orbit

Two rings built from thirty-six discrete blocks each rather than a smooth sweep, so progress reads as a count of lit segments. Steps outside, Body Battery inside.

Pick this one if you chase a daily step goal or watch Body Battery drain. The segment count is deliberate: a smooth arc hides small changes, a block either lights or it does not.

  • Steps ring 36 segments
  • Body Battery ring
  • Heart rate
  • Temperature

orbit/source/OrbitView.mc

04  Meridian

Blocky analog

Meridian

Twelve square markers instead of a sixty-tick track, square-ended hands, and a segmented Body Battery ring outside. At this pixel size a minute track turns into a grey smear, so it is left out.

Pick this one if you want a watch rather than a readout. The strip underneath is uncaptioned on purpose — colour identifies the four values once you have worn it a day.

  • Body Battery ring 24 segments
  • Hands + hour blocks
  • Steps
  • Heart rate

meridian/source/MeridianView.mc

05  Telemetry

Console readout

Telemetry

A framed panel of key/value rows with a CRT raster behind them. The strip at the bottom is a twenty-four cell day: one cell per hour, daylight lit amber, the current hour in white.

Pick this one if you want numbers and no interpretation. Five metrics, right-aligned so the digits line up as a column.

  • Heart rate
  • Body Battery
  • Steps
  • Calories
  • Floors
  • 24-hour daylight strip

telemetry/source/TelemetryView.mc

06  Horizon

Attitude indicator

Horizon

The horizon is not decoration: its height is driven by where the sun actually is, so the line sits low with a wide sky at midday and climbs over the centre mark through the night. Rungs are tinted cool above and warm below, the way a real instrument colours sky and ground.

Pick this one to read the time of day as a shape. It needs a position for the sun times, so with no weather sync it parks level and shows NO FIX.

  • Aircraft mark
  • Horizon sun-driven
  • Sky rungs
  • Ground rungs
  • Body Battery meter
  • Heart rate meter

horizon/source/HorizonView.mc

07  Trace

Oscilloscope

Trace

Recent heart-rate history as a sample-and-hold trace over a framed graticule — a step per sample rather than a sloped line, which is both the period-correct look and cheaper to draw.

Pick this one if the shape of your heart rate tells you more than its current value. The trace autoscales, which is why the window's high and low are printed beside it.

  • Heart rate trace autoscaled
  • Window high / low
  • Steps
  • Watch battery

trace/source/TraceView.mc

08  Caliper

Bar meters

Caliper

Three vertical meters of fourteen cells each. A lit-cell count is read as a height without parsing a number, and stepped cells say plainly that the reading is quantised — which it is, since all three come from counters.

Pick this one if you track three goals and want them side by side at the same scale.

  • Steps vs goal
  • Body Battery
  • Intensity minutes vs weekly goal
  • Heart rate

caliper/source/CaliperView.mc

How close this is to the real thing

Closer than the previous revision. Because the type is drawn as rectangles rather than typeset, the glyphs here are byte-for-byte the glyphs the watch will draw — the font table is generated into both this page and _shared/PixelFont.mc from one source. Positions, colours, segment counts and goal arithmetic are taken straight from the Monkey C.

What is still unverified is the device itself: none of this has been through the Connect IQ compiler, because the SDK is not reachable from the environment this was built in.

Two things no watch face can show, because Connect IQ exposes no API for them: your calendar, and last night's sleep score. Notification count is available and drives the blue status square.
